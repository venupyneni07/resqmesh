Product story
ResQMesh is an Android emergency-reporting prototype designed for connectivity disruptions, built for Hacktoberfest Hack Day Bengaluru on October 4, 2026. A person can save an SOS without internet or typing. Reachable participating devices store and carry copies; any device that later reaches the backend can upload them. The Response Center helps people review reports and AI suggestions. The current scope is a local hackathon demonstration, not a public emergency service, guaranteed rescue system or production deployment.

Mobile experience
The interface is English-only, with Home, Reports, Network and Settings. Reporter-authored text and AI transcripts retain their original language. Home offers Record voice, Take photo, Record video and Choose help. Capture starts the selected method directly, without another method chooser. Voice requires microphone permission and offers Stop & send SOS. Photo/video offer preview and explicit send. Cancellation sends nothing; failure offers retry or another method. Recording limits/interruption stop for review rather than silently sending. Capture is foreground-only.

Choose help starts an unsent draft with no preselected category. Users can choose an emergency type or Other / not sure, add Cannot move, Cannot speak or People injured, and optionally provide a people count, text and location. Review precedes sending. Drafts can be resumed or discarded. General SOS is a separate escape route with explicit confirmation showing the preset “Help needed; details unavailable.” and the exact location information to include. It explains that typed drafts, selected categories and media are not included. No category or typing is required for General SOS or media SOS.

Location may be device coordinates, a saved observation, a manual landmark or unknown. Source and observation time are preserved; stale coordinates are not presented as fresh. Denied permission does not prevent sending. Opting out prevents silent cached-location reuse. Home shows only the latest SOS; Reports shows paginated history with a Needs attention filter. Accessibility implementation includes 48dp action targets, labels/headings and reachable large-text/landscape actions. This is not proof of usability under emergency stress.

Relay architecture
Native Android Kotlin UI feeds a serialized controller, Room persistence and deterministic Kotlin relay logic. A Transport interface separates SimulationTransport from NearbyTransport. The latter uses Google Nearby Connections P2P_CLUSTER with peer authentication-code confirmation. Simulation uses a changeable graph, not a fixed A-to-B-to-C-to-D route. Devices and links may appear or disappear. Each receiving node stores the report, appends its observed route and forwards automatically. UUID deduplication, expiry, loop prevention and an eight-hop maximum bound forwarding. Duplicate copies do not establish independent witnesses.

A gateway is a device with actual backend reachability, not merely a network toggle. Gateway leases expire after 120 seconds. Multiple gateways can upload one UUID idempotently. With no usable path, reports remain locally saved, subject to forwarding expiry. AI does not choose routes. Physical relays require the app and compatible reachable peers; simulator results do not demonstrate real Bluetooth or Wi-Fi performance.

Delivery and media
Saved locally means the source stored the report. Relaying means another peer confirmed storage. Backend received requires a real backend receipt returned to the source. Human acknowledged requires a separate explicit operator acknowledgement and returned receipt. Backend acceptance and receipt creation happen before media or AI processing. Return connectivity is necessary: the backend may have the report while the source still lacks confirmation. Acknowledgement does not mean rescue dispatch or an arrival estimate.

The small SOS and attachment manifest travel first. Media follows separately between peer devices in resumable 16 KiB chunks with size and SHA-256 integrity checks. Upload direction is: gateway device sends HTTP requests TO the FastAPI backend on the laptop. Other phones transfer to the gateway through the peer Transport interface; the HTTP destination is the backend, not a gateway phone. A source that can reach the backend acts as its own gateway. Integrity is not authenticity. The report receipt does not prove attachment upload, viewing or analysis. Attachment confirmation is tracked independently from observed upload confirmation; an offline source may know the SOS arrived while its media delivery remains unconfirmed. The UI currently sends one attachment per SOS. Limits are voice 30 seconds/1 MiB, JPEG photo 1 MiB and video 15 seconds/8 MiB.

Backend and four text roles
FastAPI and SQLite persist reports, receipts, jobs, audit events and operator decisions. Local Ollama gemma4:e2b supplies real inference. Four logical roles share that model through separate prompts and validated output:
1. Intake extracts source-supported facts and uncertainty, checking quotations against original text.
2. Correlation proposes possible shared incidents for human confirmation or rejection; it never automatically merges them.
3. Verification combines observable reporting signals with AI review. It cannot decide true versus fake or prove independent sources.
4. Triage suggests urgency, response category, questions and an acknowledgement draft. It does not send that draft or dispatch anyone.
Original reports remain unchanged. People counts stay per report because victims may overlap. Durable jobs recover after restart and have bounded retries. Failed or invalid model output stays visible; there is no fabricated fallback result.

Separate media intelligence
Completed uploads enter a separate durable media worker, independent of the four text stages. Media transcription does NOT automatically feed or rerun Intake, Correlation, Verification or Triage. Audio includes at most 30 seconds. A photo contributes one normalized image. Video analysis samples only three frames, at 0%, 50% and 90% of duration, plus up to 15 seconds of audio if present. This is not continuous video understanding and can miss events.

Results include original-language AI transcription, an English summary, observations, uncertainty, suggested urgency and human checks. Provenance records model, pipeline version, time, file hash and sampling coverage. Outputs remain unverified and require human review alongside original media. States are waiting_upload, queued, running, complete and failed. Failures retry up to three times; authorized operators can retry manually. A retained result during reanalysis is labelled previous, not current.

Human response workflow
Response Center has Overview, Verification, Incoming reports and Network views, paginated lists, one selected correlation pair and case details. Current media urgency can raise displayed review priority alongside text triage. High/critical media completion produces an in-page review alert; optional browser notifications require permission and the open workspace. These alerts do not notify the reporting phone or automatically acknowledge it. Humans review originals, confirm/reject correlation, record verification, assignment, category and status, and explicitly acknowledge reports. Recorded assignment does not contact a team.

Persistence and security
Optional Background relay uses a visible foreground-service notification with Stop. Android timeouts and force-stop/Doze/OEM restrictions still apply; boot/update offers tap-to-resume, not silent activation. Recovery reuses an already-approved submission UUID without inventing a new SOS. History defaults to keep all. Optional 7/30/90-day cleanup removes only eligible old backend-confirmed reports with every attachment confirmed; pending and expired-unconfirmed reports remain.

Android Keystore AES-GCM protects durable report/receipt JSON, complete/chunk media, text/selection drafts, saved location and gateway credentials. Routing metadata remains readable. Private capture, media-draft, assembly, preview and upload files may be plaintext. This is neither full-database encryption nor end-to-end encryption or guaranteed secure erasure. Anonymous device IDs and unsigned backend receipts do not authenticate real people. Optional local accounts provide viewer/responder/admin roles, eight-hour sessions and separate node-bound gateway keys. Authenticated account identity remains distinct from self-reported reviewer labels. The verified demo used local mode without individual accounts; account checks used an isolated fixture. HTTP is not encrypted.
